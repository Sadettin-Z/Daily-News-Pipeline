### 1. France Imposes Curfews After Nationwide Riots and Looting

France imposed partial overnight curfews in parts of Paris, including Place de la République and Les Halles, and in cities including Lille, Lyon, Nice and Marseille after Saturday's nationwide protests over the cost of living and school conditions, which drew more than 500,000 people against an intelligence forecast of 200,000, erupted into rioting and looting across the country. About 2,400 to 2,500 people were arrested, around 1,000 police were injured, estimates of looted shops range from 1,500 to at least 3,000 and damage runs to hundreds of millions of euros; Limoges town hall burned and Marseille's city hall was stormed. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez admitted police had "lost control" in some cities and offered to resign, which President Macron refused, while Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said a state of emergency remained possible and promised a law making rioters pay for the damage. Macron's televised address, offering no new measures, drew criticism across the spectrum six months before the presidential election, and unions have called new strikes for 5 November.
