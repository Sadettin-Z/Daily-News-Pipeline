### 3. Binance reopens withdrawals after record $6.3bn hack; FBI blames North Korea, Bitcoin holds

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, began a phased resumption of withdrawals on Sunday after $6.3bn (£4.7bn) was stolen from its wallets on Thursday in what is believed to be the biggest hack in history; the FBI said on Saturday that North Korea was responsible and that the hackers were converting the assets into Ethereum. The company says it has replenished 90% of the stolen amount from more than $6.34bn in reserved company funds, with the less liquid remaining 10% taking longer, and co-CEO Richard Teng said "Binance will absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds, not our users." Between 06:00 and 15:30 GMT on 1 November it processed $4.649 billion in withdrawals and $1.01 billion in deposits, a net outflow of about $3.64 billion, just over 2% of the $171bn it manages, and said the initial spike was "stabilising as expected". Bitcoin held its value over the weekend, trading around $78,300, about 4% below Thursday's peak; Orbit Markets' Caroline Mauron said the market clearly believes Binance has the resources to cover the losses, whereas Bitcoin fell 19% in three days during FTX's collapse in 2022.
