### 1. Hundreds of Thousands Join France's Student-Led School Protests as Unions Rally

France's high-school protest movement, which began in mid-September over understaffed, overcrowded and dilapidated schools, held a national day of action on Tuesday, October 6, with teachers, parents, university students and unions joining for the first time and marches in more than 40 cities. The Interior Ministry counted roughly 256,000 to 266,000 demonstrators nationwide, depending on the report, and 56,000 in Paris, while student and education unions claimed 450,000. Police used tear gas in Paris and Marseille and water cannon in Rennes; the ministry reported 488 arrests and 43 injured officers on the day, with total arrests since the movement began put at 5,060 by Welt and more than 6,500 by NPR and Sözcü, most of them minors. Disruption hit 891 lycées, nearly a quarter of the total, and Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said schools would stay closed until the weekend. President Macron, who had stayed out of the protests, is reported to be convening a crisis meeting with Lecornu and the interior and education ministers; the CGT union plans further protests on Thursday.

### 2. Death of Siberian Plague Lab Technician Prompts US Pressure on Russia; WHO Rates Risk Low

Darya Shipilova, a 28-year-old lab technician at the Irkutsk Research Anti-Plague Institute in Siberia, died around October 2, two days after falling ill. The Kremlin urged people not to listen to rumours.

### 10. Francis Halzen Wins 2026 Physics Nobel for IceCube Neutrino Observatory

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Francis Halzen, 82, a Belgian-born astrophysicist at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, for leading the development of the IceCube Neutrino Observatory at the South Pole, which the Nobel committee said laid the foundations for "an entirely new type of astronomy." Halzen first proposed the project in 1988; IceCube, a cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice fitted with 5,160 light sensors, reached full size in 2011 and reported the first evidence of high-energy cosmic neutrinos in 2013.

ProPublica has tallied 506 U.S. citizens detained by immigration agents since the start of the current Trump administration, a scale far larger than previously reported and one the Department of Homeland Security denies is happening. The cases include more than a hundred Americans held after agents questioned their citizenship, nearly all people of color, at least 70 children and teens, 36 people held at least a day without access to a lawyer or family, and roughly a dozen citizens deported, mostly children. Of more than 300 citizens detained for allegedly assaulting or obstructing agents, fewer than half were charged in federal court and many of those cases were dropped. During Chicago's Operation Midway Blitz, which DHS said involved no arrests of citizens, ProPublica found more than 120 citizens detained in Illinois. DHS said its operations are "highly targeted" and that anyone obstructing agents will face arrest. The report frames the cases as evidence that the crackdown on immigrants has eroded citizens' Fourth and First Amendment rights, and notes the tally is an undercount.

### Our Reporter Spent 10 Weeks Gambling on DraftKings. Here Are 5 Things He Learned.

ProPublica reporter Jake Pearson spent 10 weeks betting on DraftKings while mimicking the behavior of a problem gambler, and found the app's responsible gaming safeguards were overpowered by product design and promotions that pushed him to keep gambling. Safeguards largely require users to opt in; his first in-app warning came only after he hit $2,500 in deposits, a prompt required by New York law, and despite depositing $21,600 in less than eight weeks he never met the company's criteria for a manual review. Instead, after three weeks and $5,800 in deposits he was accepted into DraftKings' VIP program, where a concierge later helped match a $5,000 deposit with $1,250 in free DK Dollars less than a week after he had opted into a cool-off period. Nearly a quarter of Americans say they have an active sportsbook account, a 2024 Connecticut study found 1.8% of problem gamblers accounted for 51% of sports betting revenue, and the industry has seeded a political action committee with $41 million while opposing proposed reforms. DraftKings says its system "worked the way it was supposed to."
