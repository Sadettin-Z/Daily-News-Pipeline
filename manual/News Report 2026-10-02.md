### 4. Christa Pike survives two lethal doses in Tennessee; governor halts all executions after "chemical torture"

Tennessee death row inmate Christa Pike, 50, is in critical condition in hospital "receiving life-saving care" after surviving the state's attempt to execute her by lethal injection on Wednesday, her lawyer Randy Spivey said. Pike was sentenced in 1996 for the 1995 murder of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer, whom she and then-boyfriend Tadaryl Shipp beat, tortured and killed when Pike was 18; Shipp, 17 at the time, is serving life with possible parole. After the US Supreme Court allowed the execution at the last minute, the execution team spent an hour trying to establish an IV line, using at least seven needles in her left arm; witnesses say she complained her arm was burning and asked staff to "please try up on my shoulder higher," and after two doses of pentobarbital she fell unconscious but kept breathing and snoring while observers were kept behind a closed curtain. Her lawyers made emergency calls, the execution was halted, and Spivey called it "chemical torture"; Nashville Banner reporter Steven Hale called it "a disaster by pretty much any way of looking at it." The Death Penalty Information Center says Pike is the first person in US capital punishment history to survive after being injected with lethal drugs, blaming Tennessee's secrecy, including barring media from witnessing IV insertion. The Department of Correction said it "followed every step" of a protocol that "does not allow for additional procedures" and does not specify what happens if an inmate survives a second dose; authorities have not said whether they will try again or addressed claims the drug was "degraded," and Dr Joel Zivot of Emory University said delayed resuscitation could leave Pike with a brain injury. Republican Governor Bill Lee ordered an independent review and halted all executions for the rest of the year, overlapping with Gary Sutton's planned 3 December execution, while defending the death penalty; Pike's lawyers are seeking commutation, which Lee previously denied. It was the state's second botched attempt this year after Tony Carruthers's execution was stopped in May when no vein could be found, and Pike would have been the first woman executed in Tennessee in more than 200 years. Donald Trump said "It shouldn't be very difficult"; Senator Marsha Blackburn, running for governor, called for the return of the electric chair, and Democratic opponent Jerri Green promised a moratorium. Lawyer John Mills noted the Supreme Court has historically not blocked second attempts, citing Kenneth Eugene Smith in Alabama in 2024 and Willie Francis in Louisiana in 1947. US executions rose to 47 last year, the most since 2009, with Florida accounting for 19, three by firing squad and five by nitrogen gas; of 1,683 people executed since 1976, only 18 were women. Gallup found 52% of Americans favoured the death penalty in 2025 with 44% opposed, the highest opposition since 1966; Trump ordered federal executions resumed on his first day back in office, the Justice Department declared pentobarbital the "gold standard" and added firing squad, gas and electric chair options, and Amnesty International called on the US to abolish the penalty.

### 5. Putin: Russia will use "all weapons," nuclear included, if NATO attacks or blockades Kaliningrad

### Radio signal detected from exoplanet for the first time

Astronomers report the first direct detection of radio emission from a planet outside the solar system, repeating bursts from the gas giant Beta Pictoris b, 63 light-years from Earth and about 12 times Jupiter's mass, orbiting a 23-million-year-old star 1.75 times the sun's mass. The paper, posted to ArXiv on September 15 and awaiting peer review, was led by Kevin Ortiz Ceballos with coauthor Edo Berger of the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian, using the 64-dish MeerKAT array in South Africa. The signal is auroral radio emission driven by a magnetic field Berger says is "at least 200 times stronger than the magnetic field of Jupiter," not evidence of intelligent life. The team recorded it multiple times at multiple frequencies and ruled out the host star as the source; Joseph Callingham of the University of Amsterdam, not involved, said localizing the emission to the planet is what makes the study unique. The detection went against assumptions that exoplanet fields resemble Jupiter's and emit at lower frequencies. Jonathan Nichols of the University of Leicester said if confirmed it would be a key step in understanding exoplanets' interaction with their space environment. Beta Pictoris b was discovered in 2008, with planets c and d found in 2019 and 2026; the team has requested more telescope time to study why the field is so strong.
