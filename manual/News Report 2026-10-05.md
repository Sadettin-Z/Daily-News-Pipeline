### 1. Sánchez calls snap election for 29 November after an 87-year-old's eviction and a lost housing vote; polls point to PP-Vox

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez dissolved Parliament on Monday and called an early general election for 29 November, more than six months before the term was due to end in summer 2027. The trigger was Friday's defeat of two emergency decrees from his minority Socialist-Sumar coalition: the first would have suspended evictions of vulnerable tenants until 2030, extended leases expiring before 2028 by two years, raised taxes on tourist apartments and restricted purchases by "vulture funds"; the second, more contested, included automatic renewal of rental contracts. They fell because the Popular Party, Vox and the Catalan separatist party Junts, whose seven votes the government depends on, voted against them, with Junts arguing they lacked rigour. In a televised address from Moncloa, Sánchez said "We need to mobilise and win a much larger progressive majority" to "overcome the resistance of the elites," admitted "we have made mistakes," and warned of the "extreme-right wave" seen elsewhere. The crisis was ignited by the 23 September eviction of 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal from the Madrid flat in Retiro she had lived in for more than 70 years after a legal glitch let an investment company raise her rent from €500 to €2,650, later cut to €1,650, still €300 above her pension; campaigners say the new owners later let her return at a low rent. Hundreds have camped in Puerta del Sol for more than a week, the government counted more than 70,000 marchers in Madrid on Saturday with protests also in Valencia and Barcelona, and Madrid's Tenants Union claimed about 950,000 demonstrated nationwide over the weekend. Average rents have almost doubled in a decade and asking prices have risen about 90%, according to Idealista. Sánchez, 54, in office since June 2018 through three terms of fragile coalitions without ever winning a majority, is weakened by his wife Begoña Gómez being charged with corruption in April, a May police raid on Socialist headquarters over an alleged misinformation campaign, former transport minister José Luis Ábalos being jailed for 24 years in June over Covid mask kickbacks, legal cases involving his brother, and the summer crisis in Ceuta, where thousands of mostly Moroccan migrants crossed in late July and many still sleep in makeshift camps; his government has passed no budget since 2023 even though Spain's economy has outgrown its main European peers. Internationally he is known for recognising a Palestinian state, refusing US bombers access to Spanish bases during the Iran campaign, which drew a March threat from Trump to cut off trade, and confronting "tech oligarchs." Polls put the conservative People's Party first at 32-34% against 26-27% for the Socialists and 18-19% for Vox, meaning PP leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo would need the far right to govern, the first far-right participation in central government since Franco's death in 1975. Feijóo framed the vote as "corruption or change" and said "this is about Spain beating Sánchez." Political scientist Oriol Bartomeus said housing lets Sánchez frame the vote in "classic left-versus-right terms" while the PP will campaign on corruption. A Sánchez defeat would end one of Europe's last left-leaning governments.

### 2. Flávio Bolsonaro beats Lula 47% to 45% in first round no poll predicted; runoff on 25 October with the right sweeping Congress

Brazil's presidential election goes to a 25 October runoff after far-right Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, 45, won the first round on Sunday with 47.03% (56.1 million votes) against 45.16% (53.8 million) for President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, with 99.9% counted, neither reaching the 50% needed. No poll had predicted it: pollsters had Lula three to seven points ahead, and CNN Brasil called the outcome an "electoral bomb." Bolsonaro outperformed his father's 2022 first round by nearly four points, won São Paulo and Minas Gerais, the 21-million-inhabitant state that has picked every winner since 1989 and where Lula fell to 43.21% from 48.3% in 2022, and underperformed Lula in the Northeast stronghold. Eliminated candidates, mostly centre-right and right, held 7.82% including Ronaldo Caiado (2.1%) and Renan Santos (2.2%), and about 21% of eligible voters, some 30 million people, abstained, a record; analyst Creomar de Souza said "Historically we have not seen a comeback in Brazilian runoffs," while Lula said he must convince the abstainers to make up the 2.5 million-vote gap. The right also swept down-ballot: Bolsonaro's Liberal Party won the most governors, senators and deputies, 34 of the 54 senators elected are from the right or far right, a priority since only senators can impeach supreme court justices, Michelle Bolsonaro won a Senate seat in Brasília and Carlos Bolsonaro in Santa Catarina, Jair Renan Bolsonaro a lower-house seat, Tarcísio de Freitas was re-elected governor of São Paulo with 62.64% against Fernando Haddad's 36.43%, and Sergio Moro, the judge who once jailed Lula, won the Paraná governorship. Flávio called the result "the end of an era" for the Workers' Party; Lula admitted "I was convinced I would win this election in the first round," called the choice one between democracy and "barbarism," promised to expose his rival's "dark past" and links to Rio's underworld, and accused Donald Trump of trying to swing the vote to "colonise" Brazil. Jair Bolsonaro, 71, is serving 27 years, in house arrest for health reasons, for masterminding a coup after losing to Lula in 2022 by under 2%; Flávio pledges amnesty for his father and the 8 January 2023 rioters, El Salvador-style high-security prisons with about half a million new places, a debt ceiling, spending cuts and welfare revision, while Lula touts Bolsa Família, lower unemployment, a higher minimum wage and reduced Amazon deforestation against rising living costs and debt. The campaign was shadowed by the Banco Master scandal: jailed banker Daniel Vorcaro's messages to Justice Alexandre de Moraes and a roughly 131 million real contract between the bank and the law firm of Moraes' wife came to light, Moraes denies misconduct, and Flávio admitted asking Vorcaro for 134 million reais ($25.9m) for the biopic "Dark Horse" while denying irregularities. The electoral court found no foreign interference. Trump, who hosted Flávio at the White House and imposed tariffs and sanctions over Jair's trial, has gone quiet; analysts Farid Kahhat and Manuel Alcántara Sáez said open US intervention could help Lula, who campaigns on sovereignty. A Bolsonaro win would bring Brazil into Washington's "Shield of the Americas" alliance against organised crime alongside Javier Milei and Colombia's Abelardo de la Espriella, extending a regional trend in which Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Ecuador, Colombia, Peru and Paraguay all rejected the left, driven by crime and demands for law and order. Hans-Dieter Holtzmann of the Friedrich-Naumann-Stiftung said corruption allegations on both sides deepen polarisation and leave third forces little chance. Nearly 159 million voters also chose 27 governors, 513 deputies and 54 of 81 senators; the winner takes office on 5 January 2027.

### 4. Nobel Prize in medicine goes to Deisseroth, Hegemann and Nagel for optogenetics, the light switch for nerve cells

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded on Monday, 5 October, at the Karolinska Institute in Stockholm to American psychiatrist and neurologist Karl Deisseroth, 54, of Stanford University and the Howard Hughes Medical Institute, and German researchers Peter Hegemann, 71, of Humboldt University of Berlin and Georg Nagel, 73, of the University of Würzburg, "for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics." Studying why the single-celled green alga Chlamydomonas swims towards light, Hegemann and Nagel discovered in the early 2000s channelrhodopsin, a surface protein that opens a channel under blue light so charged ions flow in and generate an electrical impulse, making any cell it is placed in light-sensitive; they first tested it in human and mouse kidney cells. Deisseroth, a former psychiatric clinician, inserted the gene into rat nerve cells, published the breakthrough in 2005 and around 2007 used optic fibres to control whisker movement in living mice, later probing memory networks including fear. The Nobel Assembly said the method "has fundamentally altered our understanding of the brain" and "laid the foundation for a new era in neuroscience," letting researchers switch individual neural circuits on or off to bring memories to life, create feelings and drive behaviours, map circuits governing pain, social behaviour, thirst, food consumption, reward and attention, and study how the nervous system interacts with organs such as the heart and gut; committee chair Per Svenningsson said it allows brain mapping "in a way that we could once only dream of," and member Anna Wedell said it yields cause-and-effect maps relevant to dementia, epilepsy and addiction. The work founded optogenetic therapy: in a clinical trial a channelrhodopsin-like protein delivered by a virus into the retina of a man blinded by retinitis pigmentosa, activated by light-emitting goggles, partially restored his sight, and others are applying it to cochlear implants, with hopes for treatments of depression, anxiety, schizophrenia, Alzheimer's and Parkinson's. Deisseroth and Hegemann won the Lasker Prize in 2021. Secretary-general Thomas Perlmann reached all three by phone and said they were "very surprised"; they called the award "absolutely wonderful" and share 12 million Swedish kronor (about $1.2 million or £900,000), to be presented with a diploma and gold medal by King Carl XVI Gustaf on 10 December. Of 235 laureates in 117 awards, only 14 have been women and none black; last year's prize went to Mary Brunkow, Fred Ramsdell and Shimon Sakaguchi. Physics follows Tuesday, chemistry Wednesday, literature Thursday, the peace prize Friday in Oslo and economics on 12 October.

### Super El Niño is here. What's so super about it?

Federal forecasters say this year's El Niño could be the strongest on record, already dubbed "Super El Niño" or "Godzilla El Niño." El Niño, which occurs every two to seven years when eastern Pacific equatorial waters warm and release heat into the atmosphere, qualifies as "very strong" when that water exceeds 2 degrees Celsius above average; NOAA places this event in that category. The last strong events were 2015-16, 1997-8 and 1982-83. Although El Niños typically peak in November and December, this one is already the strongest ever measured for this time of year, and NOAA expects it to last through early 2027. Daniel Vimont of the University of Wisconsin-Madison likened it to "an enormous rock" dropped into the Earth system. El Niño years tend to be the hottest on record; forecasters say this year may approach the top spot and records are very likely to fall in 2027, on top of warming from fossil-fuel emissions. Shifting rainfall raises flood risk in some regions and deepens drought in others, and the UN World Food Programme estimates El Niño could push at least 49 million more people into acute food insecurity by the end of 2027. In the U.S., the south faces heavier rain and flooding but possible drought relief and aquifer recharge in the Southwest, said meteorologist Martin Hoerling of the University of Colorado Boulder; the north turns warmer and drier, potentially worsening the Intermountain West drought, and California is more likely to see extreme rain. Atlantic hurricanes tend to decrease while Pacific storms intensify; Hawaii has already had several close calls. Vimont cautioned El Niño "is like loading the dice," not a guarantee, and climate change effects will persist after it fades.

### Gazze'de geçen yaz: Fare istilasındaki kamplar ve iyileşmeyen yaralar

BBC Dünya Servisi'nin Gazze haberi, Han Yunus yakınındaki El-Mawasi kampında 30 Temmuz'da görgü tanıklarına göre bir İsrail helikopterinin çadırlarını vurması sonucu dokuz yaşındaki kız kardeşi Rateel ile 20 yaşındaki kuzenini kaybeden, beyninde şarapnel bulunan altı yaşındaki Raseel'in Nasır Hastanesi'nde iki aydır süren tedavisini anlatıyor; İsrail ordusu o zaman ve yerde saldırı kaydı bulunmadığını söylüyor. Sınır Tanımayan Doktorlar'dan Dr. Avni el-Caafaravi, yaralarındaki ilaca dirençli bakteriler nedeniyle 20'den fazla kez temizleme yapıldığını ve Gazze hastanelerinde antibiyotik direncinin yüksek olduğunu, kemiğe yayılan enfeksiyonların ampütasyonları artırdığını belirtiyor. BBC'ye sunulan, henüz hakem değerlendirmesinden geçmemiş bir çalışma 2015-2025 arasında 11 hastaneden yaklaşık 80 bin bakteri örneğine dayanarak Gazze'de önemli antibiyotiklere karşı yaygın direnç buldu; Imperial College London Fleming Girişimi'nden Dr. Aula Abbara su ve arıtma sistemlerinin tahribatını temel etken sayıyor. BM'ye göre İsrail su ve sanitasyon altyapısının yaklaşık %90'ına zarar verdi; ailelerin %80'inden fazlasının su güvencesi yok, %70'e yakını kişi başı günde altı litre asgari miktarı bulamıyor. İsrail'in Cogat'ı bunu reddediyor ve ilaçların kısıtlamasız girdiğini savunuyor. Saha Yönetim Kümesi'nin Nisan araştırmasına göre yerinden edilmişlerin yaşadığı yerlerin %81'inde kemirgen veya haşere var; yaklaşık 1,45 milyon kişi etkileniyor. Uykuda gelincik saldırısına uğrayan Basel el-Dahnoun'un bacağı malzeme yetersizliği nedeniyle diz altından kesildi. Gazze Sağlık Bakanlığı, Ekim 2025'teki ABD öncülüğündeki ateşkesten bu yana 1421, 7 Ekim 2023'te başlayan savaşın başından bu yana 74 binden fazla Filistinlinin öldürüldüğünü, yaklaşık 5600 çocuğun tıbbi tahliye beklediğini açıkladı. Raseel ve babası Mahmud el-Balawi, haftalar süren İsrail onayı beklemesinin ardından DSÖ desteğiyle eylül sonunda tedavi için Mısır'a gitti.

### Riyad'da ikinci Mekke İttifakı toplantısı: Gündemde hangi konular var?

Dışişleri Bakanı Hakan Fidan, Savunma Bakanı Yaşar Güler ve Genelkurmay Başkanı Orgeneral Selçuk Bayraktaroğlu, Türkiye, Suudi Arabistan ve Pakistan liderlerinin 7 Ağustos'ta imzaladığı Mekke Ortak Savunma Anlaşması'yla kurulan Mekke İttifakı'nın Stratejik Siyasi ve Savunma Komitesi'nin (SSSK) 5 Ekim'de Riyad'daki ikinci toplantısına katılıyor; ilk toplantı 31 Ağustos'ta İstanbul'da yapılmış, genel sekreterliğin Riyad'da kurulması ve başına üç yıllığına Pakistan'ın adayı emekli Korgeneral Nauman Mahmood'un geçmesi ele alınmıştı. Toplantının odağında Suudi Arabistan ile İran destekli Husiler arasında artan çatışma var: Husi saldırıları Aramco gibi enerji altyapılarının yanı sıra Mekke ve Medine'deki kutsal mekanları da hedef aldı. Arap basınına göre Riyad daha sert askeri yanıt ve Kızıldeniz'de deniz güvenliği seçeneklerini değerlendiriyor, ABD'den hava saldırısı desteği talebine olumlu yanıt alamadı. Üç ülkenin genelkurmay başkanları 25 Eylül'de Riyad'da askeri boyutu görüşmüştü; Pakistan Dışişleri Bakanı Ishaq Dar acil toplantı duyurmuştu. Cumhurbaşkanı Recep Tayyip Erdoğan eylülde Newsweek'e tehdidin boyutuna göre askeri desteğin gündeme gelebileceğini söylemiş, Fidan askeri teknik destek verilebileceğini belirtmişti. Bakanlık kaynaklarına göre Fidan toplantıda Suudi Arabistan'a güçlü dayanışmayı teyit edecek, kutsal mekanlara yönelik provokasyonların kabul edilemez olduğunu, barışçıl çözümün ve seyrüsefer güvenliğinin önemini vurgulayacak; Suudi Arabistan'ın resmen askeri destek isteyip istemeyeceği ve Türkiye ile Pakistan'ın yanıtı bilinmiyor. Gündemde ayrıca İran-ABD barış müzakerelerinin sonuçsuz kalması, Hürmüz Boğazı'ndaki tanker geçiş sıkıntıları, İsrail'in bölgesel istikrar çabalarını hedef alması ve altı ülkenin ilgi gösterdiği genişleme var; kurucu üç ülke kurumsallaşma tamamlanmadan yeni üye alımına karşı. Anlaşma, bir üyeye yapılan saldırının diğer müttefiklere de yapılmış sayılacağı ilkesini içeriyor.
