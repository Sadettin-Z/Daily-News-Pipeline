### 3. Francis Halzen wins 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics for IceCube neutrino observatory

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Belgian-born physicist Francis Halzen, 82, of the University of Wisconsin-Madison, for conceiving and leading the IceCube Neutrino Observatory at the South Pole and discovering high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin. The detector, a cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice fitted with light sensors, was first proposed by Halzen in 1988 and completed in 2011; the Nobel committee said it paved the way for a new kind of astronomy. The prize is worth 12 million Swedish kronor, about 1.2 million dollars. The chemistry prize follows on Wednesday.
