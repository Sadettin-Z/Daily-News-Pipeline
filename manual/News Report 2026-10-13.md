### 1. Houthi missile strikes on Riyadh airport kill at least 13 and halt Saudi flights

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis have struck Saudi airports and oil facilities over several days, with a missile hitting Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on Saturday, October 10, killing 13 people and injuring more than 300; the BBC put the toll from two strikes at 14, and Bloomberg HT at at least 16, including a US citizen. Missiles were fired at Riyadh for a third straight day, killing one more person on Sunday, and the airport is closed until next Saturday, with Etihad, Qatar Airways, Gulf Air and Delta cancelling or postponing flights. The Houthis, who say they are retaliating for Saudi air strikes and a blockade after an informal ceasefire collapsed in July, warned that all Saudi airports except those serving Mecca and Medina are targets. Saudi Arabia vowed a "serious response", and Trump said the Houthis were "misbehaving very badly" and that the US could join the fight, prompting Houthi threats of a "swift and painful response" against US interests.

### 5. Cyberattack heats fridges in hundreds of French supermarkets, spoiling food and sparking fires

A cyberattack overnight from October 11 to 12 hijacked refrigeration systems in French supermarkets, raising fridge and freezer temperatures to as high as 50C and lengthening defrost cycles to delay alarms, which spoiled large amounts of food and caused some fires. Le Monde counted around 300 Intermarché-group stores, about 100 Coopérative U, 80 Carrefour and some Monoprix and E.Leclerc stores; the BBC put the total at about 400 and BBC Türkçe at about 500. The national cybersecurity agency ANSSI traced the attack to a vulnerability in software from Danish firm Danfoss, which said the affected units ran outdated firmware. Paris prosecutors opened an investigation into a "large-scale" attack, and Intermarché's CEO put losses at tens of millions of euros.
