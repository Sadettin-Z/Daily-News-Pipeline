### 1. Kremlin agrees to resume Ukraine talks around 10 November but dismisses new Western proposals

After US envoy Steve Witkoff held talks with Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg on October 21, Kremlin adviser Yuri Ushakov said Russia is in principle ready to resume talks with the US and Ukraine "around November 10", possibly in Abu Dhabi, at the technical level and focused on a "security system" for Russia and Ukraine after the war. Ushakov was dismissive of the European and Ukrainian ideas Witkoff brought from Oslo, saying "in terms of resolving the Ukraine crisis, there wasn't much there that was useful", and Le Monde reports Witkoff came away with no concessions, although he called the talks "constructive". Witkoff thanked Putin for releasing US citizen Stephen Hubbard and called him a "humanitarian", while Moscow announced its first diesel shipment to the US, a tanker with a capacity of 40,000 tonnes. Analyses by CNN and the BBC's Rosenberg argue that Putin feels no pressure to end the war, reading incentives such as Trump's lifting of sanctions on Russian diesel exports as a sign his US strategy is working while refusing to budge from demands including control over the entire Donbas region. Volodymyr Zelenskiy said he supports restarting the talks as soon as possible, and a meeting between Putin, Donald Trump and Xi Jinping may take place next month on the fringes of the Apec summit in China.

### 3. Macron hits back at Trump over French riots: 'stupid comments' from foreign leaders

Speaking in Qatar during a Gulf tour, French President Emmanuel Macron accused foreign leaders of making "stupid comments" about France's unrest for their own political ends, saying Donald Trump does not like France and distorts its troubles. Trump had claimed without evidence that France was being "conquered, looted and burned to the ground by migrants, jihadists and communists" and said the turmoil was "not about school", while Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who also faces an election, said France had been hit by an "invasion of extremist Islam". Macron said there was "no visible foreign interference" in the unrest but there had been "foreign manipulation of information", and according to the Financial Times EU regulators are examining possible disinformation campaigns, with pro-Russian accounts, Iran and Algeria suspected. Protests that began in September over education and living costs have spiralled into nationwide rioting, with more than 2,000 people arrested last weekend alone; justice minister Gérald Darmanin said 174 of the 2,500 people taken into custody had been jailed, while the BBC reported nearly 150 jailed and more than 800 brought before the courts. Vice President JD Vance and Hungarian Prime Minister Peter Magyar also linked the violence to immigration, while the unrest has pushed the yield on France's benchmark 10-year bonds past 5%, its highest since 2002.

A resumption would be the first loudspeaker exchange across the border in 16 months, raising tensions to their highest level since President Lee Jae Myung took office in June last year.
