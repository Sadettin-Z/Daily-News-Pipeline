### 1. EU leaders break off budget summit without deal on 2028–2034 spending as Merz demands all 27 states back a €45bn Ukraine loan

EU leaders ended their special summit on the 2028–2034 budget shortly after midnight on November 27 without agreement, cancelling the session planned for Friday and deferring talks to the 17 December summit, after Council President António Costa failed to bridge the camps. Costa had proposed cutting about €204bn from the Commission’s roughly €2 trillion plan, but Germany, Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, the Netherlands and Sweden – the “Sensible Seven” – said even €1.78 trillion was too much (Finland’s Petteri Orpo said cuts must reach about €250bn) and stayed away from the evening small-group meetings, while most states, many of them net recipients in southern and eastern Europe, refused further cuts to farm and cohesion funds and the Baltic states opposed a €39bn cut to defence. Chancellor Friedrich Merz said positions remain “far apart” but was confident of a deal by year-end, which the unanimous budget needs to start on 1 January 2028. Merz, who links his approval of the budget to Ukraine financing, insisted that all member states join a proposed €45bn EU loan for Ukraine in 2027 (€25bn military, €20bn civilian) because the EU “consists of 27 member states, not 23 or 24”, while Slovakia, Hungary and Czechia want to opt out, Czech premier Andrej Babiš said Ukraine funding should be negotiated separately and Slovakia’s Robert Fico accused Merz of blackmail. Costa said 24 states back the loan and Bulgaria will join only its non-military part; Berlin also wants future Ukraine funding decided by majority to shield it from a possible Marine Le Pen victory in France, and both issues return in December.
