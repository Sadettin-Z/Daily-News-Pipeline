### 1. Nationwide French cost-of-living protests turn violent with 1,200 arrests and Limoges town hall burned

Nationwide protests in France on Saturday, 17 October, against the rising cost of living and conditions in schools, which grew out of weeks of high-school protests and calls to revive the yellow-vest movement, descended into rioting, looting and arson. The interior ministry counted 550,000 marchers and the CGT union 800,000. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said 1,200 people were arrested and 150 police officers injured, up from interim tallies of 272 to roughly 400 reported earlier in the day. Limoges' historic town hall was largely destroyed by fire, protesters stormed Marseille's city hall and torched Lyon's tourist office, shops were looted in Paris, Marseille and Lille, and a teenager lost a hand to a crowd-control grenade in Saint-Étienne. President Emmanuel Macron vowed that rioters "will be prosecuted and punished" and the government blamed the violence on ultra-left troublemakers, while US Vice President JD Vance blamed it on migration policy. The unrest comes six months before the presidential election to replace Macron.

Artışın arkasında 32 trilyon doları aşan borç stoku ve beş yıl önce yüzde 1,47 olan ortalama Hazine tahvili faizinin eylül sonunda yüzde 3,52'ye çıkması var; Peterson Vakfı faiz giderlerinin GSYH'ye oranının yüzde 3,2'ye ulaşıp 1991 rekorunu aşmasını bekliyor. Ekonomistler henüz bir borç krizi beklemiyor ancak tabloyu mali kapasitenin zaman içinde aşınması olarak değerlendiriyor.
