### 1. Ukraine resumes refinery strikes as US curbs intelligence and Starlink support

Volodymyr Zelensky told the Financial Times that Ukraine will resume strikes on Russian oil refineries, ending a three-day pause it observed at Washington's request under the "energy ceasefire" Trump announced on Sunday and Russia never respected: "The block is out. We're done." He said US officials had threatened to cut intelligence sharing if the strikes continued, that Kyiv received no US early warning of Russian ballistic missile attacks two nights in a row, and that Starlink's military Starshield service was deliberately throttled, with a speed limit of about 100mph (160km/h) imposed on drone terminals on Tuesday night and connectivity restored by Wednesday evening; he will pause again only if the US supplies Patriot interceptors. Trump denied cutting intelligence, saying "We are helping them, and we are sharing intel." Elon Musk said Starshield is controlled by the US military "and ultimately POTUS" and that it is "time for Zelensky to go", echoing Trump's weekend call for a new Ukrainian president, while Dmitry Medvedev mocked Zelensky as a "green louse". The rift follows Trump's deal with Putin to import Russian diesel in exchange for suspended sanctions (300,000 tonnes according to the BBC and Welt, 1.8 million tonnes according to Al Jazeera); asked in London by Piers Morgan whether he felt betrayed, Zelensky answered "Indeed, yes", and Prime Minister Andy Burnham pledged to accelerate British air defence deliveries. Overnight Russia launched more than 850 drones, cutting power to 600,000 people in Kyiv and killing three in Kirovohrad, and Ukrainian drones set fire to the Ryazan refinery, one of Russia's largest at about 340,000 barrels a day.

### 4. China and North Korea open New Yalu River Bridge after 12-year delay

North Korea and China on Thursday opened the New Yalu River Bridge, a 3km four-lane road link between Dandong and Sinuiju and their biggest border crossing, 12 years after construction was completed in 2014. Chinese Premier Li Qiang called it a "new landmark" and a "new chapter" in ties; China accounts for about 95% of North Korea's official trade, which reached $2.7bn last year. Analysts see the opening as Beijing reasserting its sway over Pyongyang after North Korea drew closer to Russia through the Ukraine war, following Xi Jinping's June summit with Kim Jong Un, and warn the crossing could serve as a government-controlled gateway for evading UN sanctions as China's enforcement grows more passive.

### 6. EU leaders clash in Brussels over Commission's record 2028-2034 budget proposal

At the EU summit in Brussels, member states are fighting over the Commission's proposed 2028-2034 long-term budget, with Germany and the "frugal" countries demanding savings while France and others want more funds. Chancellor Friedrich Merz rejected the proposals as unaffordable for any member state, saying the draft lies 50 percent above the current medium-term financial framework and must be cut significantly; he expects "very, very difficult negotiations" and no agreement this weekend, since unanimity is far off, but hopes for a conclusion by year-end. Merz also called for a further increase in funds for Ukraine for the winter and next year and opposed transferring part of the roughly 210 billion euros in frozen Russian assets held in the EU to the United States. Marine Le Pen, leading polls for France's 2027 presidential election, denounced the push to agree the budget before next year's national elections as a "democratic scandal" and reiterated her plan to cap France's EU contribution at 5 billion euros.
